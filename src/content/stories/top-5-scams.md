Scamming has long been a practice that has affected millions of consumers worldwide. The inception of the internet dawned a completely new era of scamming, making it easier for criminals to steal your data while remaining anonymous.

However, despite internet scams becoming a prevalent fixture of internet use over the last 20 years, more people are falling for them than ever before. A [study done by TransUnion](https://newsroom.transunion.ca/suspected-digital-fraud-originating-from-canada-soars-in-2023-canada-with-third-highest-increase-in-fraud-rates-among-19-countries-analyzed-by-transunion/) revealed a 39 per cent growth rate in suspected digital fraud attempts in 2023. The same study also revealed a 258 per cent increase in the rate of suspected fraud attempts within the telecommunications sector from 2022 to 2023.

Here are the five most common scams, as identified by the [Better Business Bureau](https://bbbmarketplacetrust.org/riskreport/), so you can protect your data:

### 5. Romance Scams

Popularized by the Netflix series The Tinder Swindler, romance scams occur when the victim believes they are in a romantic relationship and agree to send money or personal information to the perpetrator. Romance scams are typically initiated through social media or dating apps.

### 4. Home Improvement Scams

One of the oldest scams continues to be one of the most common. Home improvement scams are often initiated through door-to-door solicitors who offer quick, low-cost work. They then take payment without returning or “find” issues with the work that dramatically raises the price. Home improvement scams are extremely common after natural disasters.

### 3. Online Purchase Scams

Sometimes a deal is too good to be true. Online purchase scams occur when an attractive offer is made via online marketplaces. The victim then places their order and no product or service is delivered. Sometimes the product is delivered but it is low-quality or counterfeit.

### 2. Employment Scams

Employment scams target those who are actively seeking a new job. There are many variations of this scam. In some instances, the job listing is completely fake and is used to farm your personal information. In others, recruiters may explain to the victim that they require paid “training” for the position. Employment scams also occur when someone is “overpaid” and asked to then send the difference to a different account.

### 1. Investment/Cryptocurrency Scams

Identified as the most common scam of 2023, this method pressures investors to purchase, trade or store cryptocurrency with fraudulent exchanges. Oftentimes the victim is promised near-instantaneous wealth that requires little-to-no monitoring. Another version of this scam is known as a “pump-and-dump” or “rug pull,” where a group will inflate the price of a cryptocurrency and then sell off all of their holdings which drastically deflates the price.
